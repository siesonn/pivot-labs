Six ways to start the conversation

Three questions point to the one that fits: What’s it about? Did you see it yourself? Has it come up before?

When it’s going well

Recognize what’s working

Name what they did and the difference it made, so they know what to keep doing.

Best when they did something you want to see more of.

Look ahead

Agree on one thing to work toward, and trade ideas for the next chance to try it.

Best for growth, when nothing needs fixing.

When something needs to changeGentlest to most direct ↓

Get their view first

Share what you’ve heard in neutral terms, ask what happened, and listen first.

Best when you heard about it but didn’t see it yourself.

Describe and agree

Describe what happened and its effect, hear their view, and agree on one next step.

Best for the first conversation about something to change.

Name the pattern

Link this moment to your earlier conversation, ask what’s getting in the way, and solve it together.

Best when you’ve raised it once and it has happened again.

Set a clear expectation

Say what needs to change, by when, and what happens if it continues.

Best when you’ve raised it more than once and it’s still happening.

Describe actions, not traits.

Keep what’s working separate, so it doesn’t sound like a cushion for the hard part.
