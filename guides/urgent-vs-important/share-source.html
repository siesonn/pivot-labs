Urgent vs important

Two questions sort a full list into four next steps.

Urgent
Not urgent
Important
Less important

Do now

Urgent and important

Give it your next focused block of time.

Example: a customer issue that needs an answer today.

Schedule

Important, not urgent

Protect time for it before it turns urgent.

Example: preparing for next month’s budget review.

Delegate or discuss

Urgent, less important

It needs doing soon, and it may not need you.

Example: a weekly status update a teammate is ready to own.

Reconsider

Less important, not urgent

Simplify it, defer it, or stop. Agree on the change first.

Example: a recurring meeting with no clear purpose.

Then ask: who can do it?

Important work can be handed off too. Check five things first.

  • Skills
  • Room in their workload
  • Time to brief
  • Time to review
  • OK to assign

If any answer is unclear, talk before you decide.

Idea: Dwight D. Eisenhower (1954). Four-box matrix: Stephen R. Covey (1989). The drawing and wording are Pivot Labs’ own.
