    About this tool

    The Coaching Approach Picker helps you plan one conversation to help someone grow, get unstuck, decide, get started, or re-engage. Your answers suggest one of seven approaches, and you can choose another. Most plans are built around questions, because in coaching the person you’re helping owns the answers.

    The four parts of each coaching plan (what they want, where things stand, options, and a next step) draw on the GROW™ model, developed by Sir John Whitmore and colleagues, including Graham Alexander and Alan Fine. Build on what’s working draws on the solution-focused approach developed by Steve de Shazer, Insoo Kim Berg, and colleagues, including the 1-to-10 scaling question. Weigh the options draws on the decisional balance sheet described by Irving Janis and Leon Mann. Turn it into action draws on the action and review stages of Peter Hawkins’s CLEAR model. Reconnect with the work draws on Max Landsberg’s Skill/Will Matrix. Choosing between coaching, mentoring, and giving direction, and the traps in the “Before you talk” checklist, are adapted from infographics by Amy Gibson. The asking and telling check draws on Edgar Schein’s Humble Inquiry, and the tip to ask what else comes from Michael Bungay Stanier’s The Coaching Habit®. The structure and wording here are our own.

    Some coaching methods, such as cognitive behavioral coaching and thought reframing, work with a person’s beliefs and call for specialist training. This tool sticks to conversations a leader can have well. If someone brings up something personal or health-related, listen, and point them to support such as an employee assistance program or HR.
