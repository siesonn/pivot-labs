Decision Approach Picker

How should you make this decision?

Big decision, and not sure how to make it? Describe the situation, get a suggested way to decide and why it fits, work it through, and leave with a short decision record you can share.

    About this tool

    The Decision Approach Picker helps you make one decision and record it. Your answers suggest one of four ways to decide: follow the known way, compare the options, try small and learn, or steady things first. You can choose another. The same answers always give the same suggestion.

    The four ways to decide draw on the Cynefin® framework, developed by Dave Snowden and described with Mary E. Boone in “A Leader’s Framework for Decision Making.” Compare the options is a weighted decision matrix, adapted from Stuart Pugh’s concept selection method. Its must-haves draw on the musts and wants of Charles Kepner and Benjamin Tregoe. The question about how hard a decision is to undo draws on Jeff Bezos’s one-way and two-way doors. “What could go wrong?” draws on Gary Klein’s article “Performing a Project Premortem.” The decision roles are a plain-language take on responsibility charts such as RACI and DACI. Making it hold, and the check that a decision landed, are adapted from Cicely Simpson’s infographic “The Alignment Gap.” The structure and wording here are our own.

    For decisions about people, such as hiring, promotion, or who gets an assignment, the tool asks you to set criteria before you look at the options and to use the same ones for everyone. Decisions about pay, discipline, or ending someone’s employment follow your organization’s process. Talk with HR.
