About this tool

The Eisenhower Matrix is based on the difference between urgent and important work that Dwight D. Eisenhower described in a 1954 speech. Stephen R. Covey later turned that idea into a four-quadrant matrix in The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People (1989). This Pivot Labs version adapts the quadrants and adds guided questions about timing, value, and who can do the work.

Sources: Eisenhower, D. D. (1954, August 19). Address at the Second Assembly of the World Council of Churches, Evanston, Illinois. The American Presidency Project. Covey, S. R. (1989). The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People.

A quick check-in

Choose a practical next step.

Does this work need to happen?

Consider the requirement, the outcome, and what would happen if this task were delayed or stopped.

When does work need to begin?

Considering the due date, preparation time, and anything others are waiting on, does this task need attention now? A task due next month may need work today.

Does this task need to be done by you?

Consider your authority, expertise, and judgment. You may keep final decisions or review while someone else does part of the work.

Is delegation practical?

Check the actual handoff. A possible recipient is not a confirmed owner until skills, capacity, timing, and permission are clear.
