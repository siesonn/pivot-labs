Johari Window

What do others see in you?

Pick five or six words that describe how you work. Then ask a few people you work with to pick from the same list for you. Your window shows where you agree, what others see that you didn’t name, and what you might share more.

Before you start

  • It’s your choice. Ask people you trust to be honest and kind.
  • Every word on the list is a strength. There are no wrong picks.
  • Everyone picks on their own, without seeing anyone else’s words.
  • It’s for conversation, not for reviews, ratings or hiring.
  • Nothing is saved. Print or save your window before you close the page.

    About this tool

    Based on the Johari Window, created by Joseph Luft and Harrington Ingham in 1955. The name joins their first names, Joe and Harry. The window has four boxes: what you and others both see (Open), what others see that you don’t (Blind spot), what you know that others don’t (Hidden), and what no one has noticed yet (Unknown). Luft described the Open box growing in two ways: when you share more about yourself, and when you ask others what they see. The word list, questions and wording are Pivot Labs’ own.

    Sources: Luft, J., & Ingham, H. (1955). The Johari window, a graphic model of interpersonal awareness. Proceedings of the Western Training Laboratory in Group Development. University of California, Los Angeles. Luft, J. (1969). Of Human Interaction. National Press Books.
