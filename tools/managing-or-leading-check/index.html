Managing or Leading Check

Is the work running through you, or without you?

Managing keeps the work on track. Leading builds people who can carry it without you. Both matter, and most weeks need some of each. This check looks at your last two weeks and shows where things lean right now. It’s a snapshot of your habits, not a grade. It takes about 5 minutes.

    About this tool

    The Managing or Leading Check looks at ten everyday habits in five areas (your time, decisions, handing off, direction, and growing people) and shows where your work runs through you right now. Managing keeps complex work on track: planning, organizing, checking progress. Leading sets direction and helps people act without you in the middle. Abraham Zaleznik’s 1977 article started the conversation about the difference. John P. Kotter later described the two as complementary, and said organizations need both. We follow Kotter: this check looks at what you do, not what kind of person you are. The statements and wording are our own.

    Sources: Kotter, J. P. (1990). What Leaders Really Do. Harvard Business Review. Zaleznik, A. (1977). Managers and Leaders: Are They Different? Harvard Business Review.
