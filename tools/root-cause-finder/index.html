    About this tool

    The Root Cause Finder helps you work through one problem: what happened, why, what to change, and how you’ll know the fix worked. Your answers suggest one of three methods, and you can choose another. Every method looks at what failed in the work, not who failed.

    The 5 Whys comes from Toyota. It was developed by Sakichi Toyoda and made widely known by Taiichi Ohno. The fishbone diagram, also called the cause-and-effect or Ishikawa diagram, was developed by Kaoru Ishikawa, who first used it in 1943. Three parts are adapted from Christian Rebernik’s Root Cause Analysis infographic: when a full analysis isn’t needed, the four fishbone areas, and the mistakes the tool helps you avoid. The structure and wording here are our own.

    Sources: Ishikawa, K. (1976). Guide to quality control. Asian Productivity Organization. · Ohno, T. (1988). Toyota production system: Beyond large-scale production. Productivity Press. · Rebernik, C. (n.d.). Root cause analysis: The leader’s guide to fixing problems for good [Infographic].
