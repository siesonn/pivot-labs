    About this tool

    The Time Method Picker matches what’s going on with your time to one method to try for a week. The methods are common practice. The questions, structure and wording here are our own.

    The idea of matching each method to the situation it suits comes from Sean McPheat’s “13 Time Management Methods To Develop Your Managers.”

    Where the methods come from:

    • Six things, in order: Ivy Lee, a management consultant, around 1918.
    • Most important first: Leo Babauta, who popularized “Most Important Tasks.”
    • One big, three medium, five small: Alex Cavoulacos, The Muse (2013).
    • Hardest thing first: Brian Tracy, Eat That Frog! (2001).
    • Short focus rounds: Francesco Cirillo, the Pomodoro® Technique (late 1980s).
    • Put it on the calendar: timeboxing, a common practice with no single originator.
    • Set a shorter deadline: C. Northcote Parkinson, “Parkinson’s Law,” The Economist (1955).
    • Do it now if it takes two minutes and One trusted list: David Allen, Getting Things Done® (2001).
    • Group them: task batching, a common practice.
    • Big things in first: Jeremy Wright, “Time Management: The Pickle Jar Theory,” A List Apart (2002).
    • The few that matter most: the Pareto principle, named by Joseph M. Juran after the economist Vilfredo Pareto.
    • Sorting by urgent and important: the Eisenhower Matrix, which has its own tool here.
